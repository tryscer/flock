*(tssssssssssss—)*

Slowly, slowly, I let the needle descend unto the vinyl. I’ve been told very sternly I should never let the needle scratch the record, that the tip has to find its way exactly into the groove, to nestle in it. For some reason, that image fills me with strange, alien feelings. 
​	I’m sixteen years old. It’s Christmas eve. 
​	The needle finds its groove. The little speakers on the turntable emit piano notes I’ve never heard before —  and then a voice, manly but soft, afflicted with Southern twang, strong, bathed in emotion, comes on...

*(Ibeleev —)*

The driving rhythm behind the soft music does something to my hips. At first they sway, as if on their own, like children do when you play to them. I want to dance but I’m ashamed. Then, those strange, alien feelings, like tingles, spread through the floor of my stomach. 

*(Inthewohords —)*

I feel as if I’ve barged into a room I was not supposed to be let into. The notes coming from the little speaker are little pockets of illicit, frightening delights. 
​	I lift the needle. This is all too much, and too soon. 

*(Let’s hear it for our new stage hand, Tommy —)*

I’m drenched in sweat. I’m in the drive-in that has been transformed into a huge rock concert. I feel the pressure of the crowd around me, pushing me closer to the stage. I look up at Tim on stage, his white shirt almost translucent and clinging to his body, standing among the giant Steam Pillars as if he’s of their own. 
​	I’m almost 18. I’ve just discovered the Big Secret. I’m dying for Tim to get off the stage so I could share it with him when he struts forward, as sure as a king. He looks at the crowd like a king. A small murmur begins around me. 
​	Slowly, Tim lifts his hands in triumph. I can see the lines of his muscles stretch and flex. The murmur becomes a cheer. His arms drag the wet shirt up across his stomach and in the light of the headlights — almost like spotlights now — I see his naval, a small line of orange hair going down into his pants. I feel the tingles going through my belly, reverberating through me, pulsing with the beat of the drums. They are no longer alien or strange. Someone near me yells —

*(Who’s that DISH?)*

I yell, “He’s *my* dish!”
​	I feel overcome with a desire to kiss Tim, to crowd him and squish him. It goes frantically through me. I blow kisses at him but it isn’t enough. I turn to the man next to me and yell, “I don’t know who you are, but I have to dance with someone right now!”
​	Tim and the Pillars are towering right above me. I can smell their sweat and the electricity in the air and the rubber of their truck’s tires.
​​	Heath slams the piano keys one last time. The band all joins in in singing the last refrain —
